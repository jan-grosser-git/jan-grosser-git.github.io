Master Thesis
Assessing the robustness and replicability of experimental findings on fairness preferences and their determinants

The objective of this thesis is to evaluate the replicability of previous experimental studies. By employing a unified estimation approach, this study addresses the challenge of comparing fairness preferences and evaluates the robustness of heterogeneous fairness ideals in diverse contexts. This thesis confirms the necessity for reproducible research, although the findings are predominantly qualitatively replicated, with some discrepancies.

Bachelor Thesis
Assessing binary classification methods in credit scoring: A comparison of econometric and machine-learning techniques, tested on an authentic data set

In this thesis different methods, some of which are used in application and others which are rather rudimentary, are compared and evaluated using well-known metrics. The data set, is , unlike many others used in this field, not synthetic and therefore very suitable to compare the methods. Two older methods, linear discriminant analysis (LDA) and logistic regression (LR) achieve better results, than more advanced and modern methods.
